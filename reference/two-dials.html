The two dials

Quick reference. Prints on one or two sheets. Taught in Lesson 02.

The model in four lines

  • Dial 1 — addressee. 丁寧語, the です・ます layer. Lives on the sentence ending. Deference to whoever is listening.
  • Dial 2 — referent. 尊敬語 raises them, 謙譲語 lowers me. Lives in the verb stem. Positions the people in the sentence.
  • They are independent. Every combination is legal and each means something distinct.
  • Plain ≠ informal. Polite ≠ formal. A spec in である is formal with Dial 1 at zero, because it has no listener.

The grid — 行く

Dial 2 neutralDial 2 raise themDial 2 lower me
Dial 1 plain 行く いらっしゃる 伺う
Dial 1 polite 行きます いらっしゃいます 伺います

Top-right is the cell English speakers forget exists: honorific about someone, casual toward your listener. Talking to a friend about your boss.

謙譲語Ⅰ or Ⅱ? — the one-question test

Is there a person in this sentence whose status I am raising by lowering myself?

  • Yes → 謙譲語Ⅰ. 部長のところに伺います — the visit has a respectable target.
  • No → 謙譲語Ⅱ. 大阪に参ります — Osaka cannot be respected. The humility is aimed at your listener.

This is exactly why the three-category model was replaced: it had no slot for the second case.

内 / 外 — the flip

Speaking to田中部長 isYou say
A colleague inside ミナト外 relative to you — senior 田中部長はいらっしゃいます
A client at ケイヨウ内 — your side now 田中はおります

Dial 1 stays high in both — you are polite to whoever you are addressing. Only Dial 2 flips, because the boundary of "my side" moved. Drop the title and the さん when your own people cross to 内.
