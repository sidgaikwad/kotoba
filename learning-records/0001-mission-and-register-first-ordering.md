# Mission established: professional register, not JLPT

The learner is a developer aiming to work in a Japanese engineering organisation. The stated goal is
**social operability in Japanese at work**, not exam performance. He explicitly identified register as
the thing he will underestimate and asked to be corrected aggressively on it.

## Implications for ordering

This justifies a deliberate departure from the conventional syllabus: **the politeness axis is
introduced very early**, before conditionals, before most N4 grammar, and in parallel with kana rather
than after it.

The reasoning: register errors are *silent*. Grammar errors get corrected by colleagues because they
impede comprehension; register errors do not impede comprehension, so nobody mentions them, and the
learner never self-corrects. Time spent on a silent-failure skill is worth more than the same time
spent on a loud-failure skill, because the loud one has a natural feedback loop and the silent one has
none.

Corollary recorded so it is not re-litigated later: kana and vocabulary are *volume* problems that the
app's SRS will solve without much teaching. Register is a *model* problem that needs teaching. Bias
lesson time accordingly — let the app grind the volume, spend my lessons on the model.

## Sourcing decision recorded here because it steers every future lesson

Copyrighted textbooks are excluded by the learner's own instruction. The replacement spine is
敬語の指針 (文化庁, 2007) for register, plus openly-licensed corpora (JMdict, KANJIDIC2, KanjiVG,
Tatoeba) for content the app can legally ship. This is not a compromise — the government keigo report
is a *better* source for this mission than any textbook, because textbooks still teach the superseded
three-category model.
