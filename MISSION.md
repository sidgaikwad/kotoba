# Mission: Japanese for working as a software engineer in Japan

## Why

I want to work as a developer in a Japanese engineering organisation and function there socially, not just technically. Passing the JLPT is not the goal — being someone my team can work with in Japanese is. The gap I most need closed is **register**: knowing which version of a sentence to use with which person, in which channel, at which moment.

Secondary and real: I want friendships too, not only a working vocabulary. The same person needs to hear the right register at 15:00 in a design review and at 20:00 at a 飲み会.

## Success looks like

- I follow what my team lead actually says in a 朝会 at natural speed.
- I write a Slack message that reads as written by a person, not translated from English.
- I ask a senior engineer for help without sounding rude *or* grovelling.
- I disagree with a design decision without damaging the relationship.
- I apologise at the severity the mistake actually warrants — no more, no less.
- I write a 障害報告 a Japanese manager accepts without rewriting.
- I read Qiita / Zenn / 仕様書 at working speed.
- Given an unfamiliar situation, I can reason out the right utterance instead of recalling a memorised one.

## Constraints

- I am a developer: don't explain programming. Do over-explain social mechanics.
- Near-beginner in Japanese; **complete** beginner in Japanese workplace norms.
- Short lessons with one insight beat long chapters.
- Correct me aggressively, especially on register. An uncorrected politeness error is one I'll repeat in front of a future team lead.
- The course is built into an app we are also building (macOS desktop, offline-first). Teaching and building reinforce each other.
- No copyrighted textbook content. Openly-licensed and primary sources only.

## Out of scope

- JLPT exam-technique coaching for its own sake. Levels are a *scope map*, not the target.
- Handwriting production. Recognition and typing only, unless this changes.
- Keigo trivia that no working engineer uses (I want the register that is actually spoken in offices in the 2020s).
- Kansai-ben and other regional variation, for now.
